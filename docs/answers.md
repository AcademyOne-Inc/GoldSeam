# How to read a GoldSeam answer

Every answer is built to be checkable. Four things are always true.

## 1. An answer names its source

A school's own catalog page and the edition it came from, a published rule, the federal record, or the
issuing body. If a statement has no source behind it, GoldSeam does not make it.

**If your AI tells you something GoldSeam did not say, that part is not from GoldSeam.** Ask it which
tool answered and what the source was — the answer carries both.

## 2. An answer says which snapshot produced it

Catalogs change. Every answer carries the **contract tag** that produced it (`courseshelf_v1`,
`goldcheck_v1`, `goldribbon_v1`, `industry_certifications_v1`, `transitions_v1`, `ksa_dictionary_v1`)
and the date of the capture behind it, so you always know how current it is and can say so to someone
else.

## 3. "not held" is an answer

| You see | It means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **not held** | we have not captured that yet | it is an honest gap, not an opinion. Say so, and [tell us](../README.md#support) |
| **offline** | the catalog service was unreachable at that moment | try again shortly |

An absence is stated. It is never filled in from memory, and it is never softened into a guess. A
missing title or description stays empty rather than being replaced with a placeholder.

## 4. Nothing is invented

GoldSeam will not name a school, a program, a course, a requirement or a credit rule that is not held.
It will not average across schools, rank them, or decide something an institution decides. Where a
judgment belongs to a school, the answer gives you what the school published and says whose call it is.

## Asking well

- **Name the school.** Most questions are about one institution's own record; a UNITID or a full name gets a better answer than "colleges near me".
- **Use the person's own words first**, then follow the ids the answer returns. Ids come from earlier answers, never from guesswork.
- **Ask for the source.** It is always there, and it is the difference between an answer you can act on and one you cannot.
